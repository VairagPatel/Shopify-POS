CHAPTER 01

The problem we are solving

Eluno needs a better billing experience in its stores while keeping Shopify as the official record of customers, products, inventory, orders, recorded payments and refunds.

The assignment describes seven Bengaluru stores. Staff already use an internal store tool for queues and prescriptions, but billing happens in Shopify POS. The problem is the gap between those systems and the store’s daily work.

At the counter

Staff need quick billing, correct offers, customer flags and one bill for a family. Manual work makes the experience inconsistent.

After the sale

A wrong lens creates a remake. A return can put stock in the wrong location. A lost response can tempt staff to charge twice.

At day-end

Finance needs correct tax, traceable invoices, recorded drawer expenses and numbers that agree with Shopify. Leadership needs the whole day’s performance.

Explain it this way

“We are building the billing layer between the store team and Shopify. It brings the customer, prescription, product, payment and reporting steps into one controlled process.”

What success means

  • A valid bill produces one payment attempt and one recorded source order, with exact amounts and traceable references.
  • An invalid lens or insufficient stock is caught before payment.
  • A full return reverses the original money and tax, and restores the frame to the right store.
  • Staff and leadership can explain the result using the same source records.

These are acceptance goals. We have not measured a reduction in remakes, conversion improvement or live-store time savings. Those business outcomes need a pilot.

CHAPTER 03

The steps we took to build it

This is a reconstruction from the code, conversation and test evidence. It describes the work performed; it is not an invented sprint history, timesheet or proof that every activity happened in a strict order.

  1. Read the brief and stakeholder inputs.

    Extracted the seven Must flows, the store/finance/clinical conflicts and external dependencies. Prepared the stakeholder reply and recorded assumptions.

  2. Inspected the supplied data and mocks.

    Identified seven stores plus HQ, source catalog/stock/customer records, store-tool prescriptions/staff and the payment terminal. The exercise explicitly permits these mocks.

  3. Built business rules and local state.

    Created cart, prescription, lens, tax and discount logic; added saved quotes, state transitions and store invoice allocations in SQLite.

  4. Connected the financial flow.

    Added source reads, terminal charge, paid source order, full refund and source-derived day-end report. Kept uncertain writes visible instead of automatically repeating them.

  5. Added staff workflows and the two interfaces.

    Built login, roles, store boundaries, family billing, clinical entry, remakes, cash operations and the frontend. Swagger exposes the same backend process for a technical demonstration.

  6. Tested the assumptions and fixed defects.

    The cashier review exposed the one-frame restriction. Tests also found malformed-request crashes, invalid money input and a cash-refund retry issue. We corrected them and checked the source effects.

  7. Improved leadership visibility.

    The Reports page now starts with company-wide daily totals. Store rows are optional detail. The outbox now shows report tables, readable timestamps and honest delivery status.

  8. Prepared the handoff.

    Added test plans, walkthroughs, architecture notes and recording guidance. The initial repository was pushed to GitHub. The latest report/outbox changes and this document remain local until another push.

AI assistance: AI helped with implementation, tests, investigation and documentation. An early assisted design assumed one frame/lens pair per wearer; real cashier feedback and regression checks exposed that limitation. The code still needs human understanding, review and business sign-off. Do not describe AI-generated work as independently handwritten.

CHAPTER 05

Where the data comes from

We do not read data from the Shopify POS screen. The backend calls Shopify-style APIs through an adapter. The exercise provides local versions of Shopify Admin, the store tool and a terminal. All records and payments in this demo are synthetic.

Arrows show requests from the backend; responses return on the same connection. A “mock” is a simulated external service used to exercise the real application flow without touching live customers or money.

CHAPTER 06

The complete billing flow

Example: an existing customer wants a frame and prescription lens at HSR. The cashier confirms the customer, adds the correct items, reviews the quote and accepts payment.

Identify→Validate→Price→Save quote→Charge→Write order→Read back
StepWhat the system doesWhat exists after this step
1 · Sign inAuthenticate the user; check session, role, store ownership and CSRF on writes.A known staff identity, not yet a bill.
2 · Identify customerLook up source customer by phone; read tags and latest valid prescription from the store tool.The payer, wearer/Rx and relevant customer flags.
3 · Select itemsCashier chooses source variant IDs for each wearer. Repeated IDs represent units; frames/lenses pair in their selection order per wearer.A draft cart. Up to ten wearers and thirty units each in this demo.
4 · ValidateBackend fetches variants, staff and location; checks combined stock demand, Rx expiry and lens compatibility.Either a clear rejection before payment, or a valid cart.
5 · Calculate and saveEvaluate configured offers; split inclusive GST per line; reserve store invoice number; save immutable quote and prepared order payload.checkout_id, invoice_no, total_paise, tax_paise, offer decisions and draft state. No payment, order or stock reservation yet.
6 · ConfirmReload the saved quote and recheck stock. Persist pending payment reference before attempting the terminal charge.Captured charge reference if successful, or an uncertain state to investigate. Cash needs an open drawer and bypasses the electronic terminal.
7 · Write the source orderAdd successful payment transaction to the saved payload; POST the paid order. The source applies tracked inventory decrement.Source order ID linked to our checkout, order_recorded state and issued invoice.
8 · Show proofFetch the actual source order; display invoice, payment reference, tax/discount lines and later refunds.Evidence that the bill reached the source, not merely a successful local calculation.
9 · Include in reportsRead source orders and transactions for the selected day and stores.Store report and combined leadership totals. No separate copy of the sales ledger is used as final proof.

What goes back into Shopify?

The items and amount

  • Customer and billing location IDs.
  • Variants, quantities and inclusive prices.
  • Discount applications and line allocations.
  • Tax lines and lens Rx / wearer properties.

The audit trail

  • Successful sale transaction and charge reference.
  • Our checkout ID and store invoice number.
  • Store, staff, channel and payment method.
  • Manager reason/approver or remake reference where relevant.
Explain it this way

“Fetching the customer is just the start. We validate and price the bill, save it, accept payment, write the paid order back, and then read the order again to prove it is there.”

Three different IDs: the checkout ID tracks our operation; the payment charge ID tracks money at the terminal; the Shopify order ID tracks the source sale. The store invoice number is another separate reference. These must not be confused.

CHAPTER 08

Returns, remakes and daily operations

Full return

Reverse money, tax and inventory

  1. Manager selects the original recorded checkout and reason.
  2. Read original source order; ask source to calculate refund.
  3. Verify full amount and original tax.
  4. Refund terminal money, or handle cash return.
  5. Write source refund with original transaction and line references.
  6. Restock the frame at the billing store; do not restock the lens.
  7. Show linked credit-note evidence.

A source refund record and a terminal refund are separate calls. Full refunds are implemented; partial returns are not.

Remake

Replace a lens without rebilling the frame

Link a new lens-only checkout to the original invoice and wearer. Retain the reason, approving identity, lab cost and who bears that cost.

For company/external-optometrist cost allocation, customer charge can be zero. Customer-funded work can require payment. No new frame line or frame stock movement is created.

An external optometrist’s liability is recorded; automatic recovery of money from that party is not implemented.

Cash drawer

Make the closing count explainable

Opening float + cash sales − cash refunds − expenses = expected cash.

Example: ₹2,000 float + ₹9,000 sales − ₹300 expenses = ₹10,700 expected. If the cashier counts ₹10,450, variance is −₹250. That exceeds the absolute ₹200 threshold, so the app queues a finance alert.

Pending financial exceptions block close. A queued alert is not evidence that finance received an email.

Access

Give each role the right actions

Cashier sells and records expenses. Manager handles overrides, returns, remakes and drawer close. Optometrist authors prescriptions. Finance sees cross-store reporting/audit/outbox. Admin has central access.

The API enforces permissions, not just hidden buttons. Store-scoped users cannot read another store’s checkout by guessing its ID.

The clinic and the lab are still separate systems

This project validates billing choices and retains clinical/remake references. It does not replace the lab order-management system, QC, dispatch, the queue application or camera hardware. We should not present those as delivered modules.

CHAPTER 14

How to explain it in a meeting

A simple opening

“The problem is not only making a bill. It is making sure the correct customer, prescription, price, tax, payment and stock movement stay connected. I built an Eluno till layer that keeps Shopify as the source record and controls that full journey.”

What to showWhat to sayWhat proves it
Source customer, Rx and stock“These are existing source records. No sale has happened yet.”Customer tags, prescription limits, variant and location IDs.
Prepared quote“We validated the cart and calculated offers and inclusive GST before payment.”Draft checkout, invoice allocation, total and offer reasons.
Completed sale and source detail“The terminal charge came first; then we wrote the paid order back.”Charge reference, actual source order, tax/discount/Rx properties and stock change.
Full refund“We reverse the original amount and tax, returning the frame only to the store that sold it.”Source refund and before/after location inventory.
Daily reports“Leadership sees the company total first; store detail is optional.”Combined metrics for chosen date, with source-backed definitions.
Report outbox“This is what is prepared for leadership or finance; the status tells us whether SMTP accepted it.”Report table, timestamp, saved body and delivery state.
Tests and limits“We tested the financial rules and failures. These are the boundaries still needing a pilot.”Recorded runs, regression cases and open-gate list.

Use http://127.0.0.1:8001 for the app and /docs for Swagger while the local runner is active. Admin/finance see central reports. HSR cashier credentials demonstrate store restrictions. Do not show passwords during recording.

Technical appendix: exact API chain
App entry pointExternal work / result
GET /api/bootstrap and /api/inventory; POST /api/customersSource locations/products, customers/search, inventory_levels; store-tool prescriptions/staff.
POST /sales/prepareRead source records; validate; calculate; save quote/invoice locally. No terminal charge or source order POST.
POST /sales/{checkout_id}/completePOST /terminal/charges for UPI/card, then POST source orders.json with the transaction.
GET /api/checkouts/{identifier}/detailLoad saved quote/state and GET source orders/{id}.json for evidence.
POST /refunds/prepareRead original order and POST orders/{id}/refunds/calculate.json; verify totals; save prepared refund.
POST /refunds/{refund_id}/completePOST /terminal/refunds (electronic), then POST orders/{id}/refunds.json with tax/stock/transaction treatment.
GET /reports/day-end or /api/reportsRead source orders.json and aggregate by the documented store/date basis.
POST /api/reports/digest; GET /api/outboxCreate durable report message; show saved preview and delivery status. Scheduler can submit to configured SMTP.
